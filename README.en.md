# PCManager — how to install and use

> **Português:** [README.md](README.md)

> Up to version 0.1.4 the app was called **HDManager**. If you already had it installed, the update brings the new name, and the installer removes the old installation on its own (your history and settings stay).

PCManager shows your PC piece by piece, in the sidebar on the left: first the parts (**Storage**, **Hardware** and **Apps**), then the topics (**Games**, **Internet** and **Windows**). On the disk, it shows where the space went: which folders and files take up the most, what each folder is ("what is this?") and what is probably left over from uninstalled apps, abandoned projects and games you haven't opened in months. **It never deletes anything on its own**: in **Cleanup → Temporary files** you choose which caches and temporary files to move to the Recycle Bin (or delete for good), in **Cleanup → Duplicates** which repeated copies to move to the Recycle Bin, in **Installed** which games and apps to compress, in **Files** which folders and files to move to the Recycle Bin, and to uninstall an app or game it opens the official uninstaller.

## Install

1. Download the `PCManager_<version>_x64-setup.exe` file of the newest version from https://github.com/abreusleo/HDManager-releases/releases/latest (under **Assets**).
2. Windows may show **"Windows protected your PC"** (SmartScreen). This happens because the app has no paid digital signature. Click **More info** and then **Run anyway**.
3. Accept the administrator permission prompt. It shows up during the installation and **every time you open PCManager**, not just the first time. The app needs it to read the disk index (the NTFS MFT). It's the same method WizTree and Everything use, and it's what makes the scan take seconds instead of minutes.
   - If you click **No**, Windows simply doesn't open the app. That's expected; open it again and accept.
4. If your Windows doesn't have WebView2 (the Microsoft component the app window uses), the installer downloads it on its own.

## Use

- **Sidebar:** **Home** stays fixed at the top and, below it, your **Favorites** (click the star in the title of any screen or tab, or when you hover over an item, and it stays pinned there). The other screens are in six groups, and each item has tabs with its steps: **Storage** (Disks: overview, growth and health; Files: folders and search; Cleanup: suggestions, temporary files, duplicates and old drivers; Organize), **Hardware** (Sensors, Memory and, on a laptop, Laptop and Battery), **Apps** (Installed: apps, updates, startup and Windows apps; Activity: now and usage time), **Games** (Sessions, Optimization and Ping), **Internet** (Connection: speed, stability and usage by app; Fixes: DNS and repairs) and **Windows** (Checkup; Performance: tweaks, services, tasks and boot; Preferences: privacy, personalization, keyboard and OneDrive; Maintenance: Windows Update, errors and repairs; Changes). Click a group's name to close or open it (the screen you're on stays visible); the small round arrow on the edge of the sidebar leaves it with icons only. When you hover over an item, a small eye shows up: clicking it moves the item to the **Hidden** group (at the end of the list), and the crossed-out eye puts it back in its group. The first time, a short tutorial shows the main parts (you can see it again in **Settings → About**).
- **ⓘ How it works:** next to the title of each screen and the buttons that change something, the **ⓘ** explains in a few lines what the app does, what it never touches and which Windows command or function it uses. Everything is gathered in **Settings → How PCManager works**.
- **Don't touch:** Windows files and folders and those of installed apps (virtual memory, recovery environment, installer copies, your account's registry…) show up marked in red with the reason. The app never deletes any of them, and the features that move or delete files don't accept those folders.
- **First time:** pick a disk. On an SSD the scan takes a few seconds; on a hard drive or flash drive, longer.
- **Language:** the app follows the Windows language: Portuguese on a Portuguese Windows, English on any other. To switch, go to **Settings → Language** (it changes right away, no restart). Names that come from Windows, like devices and services, stay in the Windows language.
- **Home:** the screen that opens first, the state of the PC at a glance. At the top, badges with the free space on C: and today's internet drops. Below, the **Now** strip with the CPU, the graphics card and the memory in live gauges; the **disks** with their space and how much they grew in 7 days; the **internet** with the last test and, with Stability on, the drops of the last 24 h; and **You can free up**, with what Cleanup found. Nothing new goes to the internet when it opens.
- **Disks → Overview (Storage):** the donut shows usage by category (Games, Apps, Dev, System, Cache…). The cards show orphans, forgotten files, dev junk, games not played and system items. Click a card to see the items. The **Recycle Bin** card shows how much it takes up and has **Empty** (Windows asks for confirmation).
- **Files (Storage):** tree on the left, chart on the right (Treemap, Sunburst, By category or **By type**, which adds up by extension: `.mp4`, `.iso`… click one to search the disk). Double-click opens the folder; Backspace goes back. The bar at the bottom explains the selected item and has **Open in File Explorer** and **Copy path**; on system items (virtual memory, restore points…) it also has the button that opens the right Windows screen.
- **Installed (Apps):** installed apps and games, from largest to smallest, with the **Uninstall** button. It opens the app's own uninstaller, or Steam or Epic (for games). The uninstaller of an app installed only for your account (Zoom, Discord, Arduino…), or of an app whose uninstaller sits in a folder any app can change, opens as you, without PCManager's administrator rights (if it needs them, Windows itself asks); if its command can't be read, Windows Settings opens. Anti-cheats (Vanguard, Easy Anti-Cheat…) have no button: uninstall them through the game. Nothing is uninstalled during a game. When it finishes, you see how much space was freed. Check several and use **Uninstall N** to uninstall in a batch: one at a time, in order, with **Skip this one** and **Stop the batch**; the **Without prompts** option (off by default) uses the silent mode the app itself registered, when there is one. After uninstalling, the **leftovers** show up (the app's folder, folders with the same name in AppData, ProgramData and Program Files, and broken shortcuts) to move to the Recycle Bin; what is outside your user folder comes unchecked, and for an app installed only for your account only what's inside that folder shows up; the registry and folders of other apps never go in.
- **Top bar:** on every screen. On the right, **Notifications** and the **long tasks** in progress (disk scan, Cleanup, Duplicates, Compress, Speed test and Check Windows files), with the time; click one to go back to its screen. Notifications have three parts: at the top, **You can turn on** (what's off by default and may be useful: Assistant, Internet stability, Game sessions, Usage time and Alert when an app hogs resources); then **To improve** (the Checkup list, each with a button to fix it) and **What happened** (today's internet drops, with Stability on, tasks that finished while you were on another screen and, after a Windows update, the PCManager tweaks it undid, with **Apply again** and **See in Changes**). **Turn on** takes you to the screen where the feature is explained and turned on; nothing turns on by itself. **Not now** (×) removes the item for good. The number adds up what can be improved, what you haven't seen yet and what's new in You can turn on; closing Notifications marks them as seen.
- **Ctrl K:** from any screen, type the name of a screen, an action or a file (accents optional). Actions that only read or measure run right away (Run a checkup, Test the internet speed, Check the health of the disks, Open the sensors window); the ones that change the PC (Empty the Recycle Bin, Clean up, Repairs, Uninstall) only open their screen, where you confirm.
- **Search (Storage → Files):** through `Ctrl K` or the **Search** screen. It accepts `*` and `?` (e.g. `*.mp4`) and filters: `size:>1gb`, `ext:mp4,mkv`, `modified:<2024`, `modified:>30d` (more than 30 days ago), `type:folder`. With an empty search, it shows the 1000 largest files on the disk.
- **Cleanup (Storage):** three tabs for the same question, "what can I delete?": **Suggestions** (orphans, forgotten files, dev junk, games not played and what belongs to the system), **Temporary files** and **Duplicates**.
- **Temporary files (Storage → Cleanup):** temporary files, browser and app caches (checked by default), shader cache, dev package caches and dev junk from abandoned projects (optional). Choose to move them to the **Recycle Bin** (can be undone; the space comes back when you empty the Recycle Bin) or **delete for good**. Files in use are skipped and listed. Nothing is cleaned during a game.
- **Duplicates (Storage → Cleanup):** finds files with the same content in your folders (or on the whole disk), without looking at games, apps, Windows and AppData. It compares the size first, then the beginning and the end, and only reads in full what tied; it runs in the background, pauses on its own during a game and doesn't download OneDrive files that are only in the cloud. You check the copies (or use "Keep the oldest"/"Keep the newest"); at least one always stays, and they go to the **Recycle Bin**.
- **Move to Recycle Bin (Storage → Files):** select an item, or check several with **Ctrl+click** in the tree, and use **Move to Recycle Bin** in the bottom bar. First, the app shows how many items and how much space they take. You can restore them from the Recycle Bin, and the space comes back when you empty it; if something is too big for it, Windows asks before deleting for good. Locked: the Windows folder, the roots of Program Files, ProgramData and Users, each account's folder and its AppData, everything inside other accounts' folders, your Downloads, Documents, Pictures, Videos, Music and Desktop folders (what's inside them is fine), system files, PCManager's data, anti-cheats and the folder of an installed app or game (use Uninstall or the launcher). In **Likely orphans** (Suggestions) each folder also gets the button. Nothing is deleted during a game.
- **Compress (Apps → Installed):** large games and apps get the **Compress** button, which uses Windows' own compression (the same as `compact /EXE:XPRESS8K`). It's transparent: the game opens and runs the same, and the file contents don't change. First, the app shows how much it should free (usually 20% to 50%). It runs in the background, pauses if a game opens and can be canceled. Games with anti-cheat (Easy Anti-Cheat, BattlEye, Vanguard) ask for one extra confirmation. After a game update, the new files arrive uncompressed: the button becomes **Compress the rest**. **Decompress** undoes everything.
- **Background (Settings):** everything PCManager does while you're not looking, with an on/off switch. Only one small PCManager service starts with Windows (`hdmanager-uso.exe`), and only if you turn on Usage time, Internet stability or Alert when an app hogs resources; the list shows whether it's running, since when and how much it uses. It also lists what runs only with the app open (live mode, History snapshots, low space alert and **Check the tweaks after Windows updates**, which is on by default: when the app opens and every 6 h, it only reads and tells you in Notifications what an update undid).
- **Internet stability (Internet → Connection):** starts off. When on, the service sends a ping every 2 s to your router and to the internet and shows packet loss, ping and jitter on a timeline in two lanes, from 15 minutes (each ping) to 7 days (the router and the internet), with a diagnosis: lost even at the router → Wi-Fi, cable or router; only on the internet → provider. In **What happened**, the screen recognizes on its own a router that stopped answering, an internet drop with the router working, periods of loss and a router that seems to have restarted; **Copy report** builds a text with the times to send to your provider. If Usage time is also on, it shows how the internet was during each game. You can measure only while a game is open. A few MB a month, without touching game traffic.
- **Game server ping (Games → Ping):** starts off. When on, with a game open and PCManager open (in the window or in the system tray), the app listens to Windows network events only for the game (address and packet size, never the content), finds the server and pings it once a second. It shows up as one more lane in **Stability**, with the note "the delay was on the way to the game server" when the router and the internet were fine, in **Game sessions** (the **server** column, next to **internet**) and as a piece on the Sensors board (**Game** group). Many servers don't answer ping: then the app measures up to the last point on the route that answers, near the server, and says so. It doesn't open the game process, doesn't inject anything and doesn't install a driver.
- **Speed test (Internet → Connection):** in **Test now**, a speedometer shows the current speed while it measures download, upload, ping and jitter against Cloudflare's public servers, in about 15 seconds, and keeps the history. Only when you click and never during a game. It uses data: on a 1 Gbps connection, about 600 MB per test.
- **Usage time (Apps → Activity):** how long each app and game was in the foreground, by day and by hour. Starts off: in **Usage time → Turn on**, the background service starts with Windows (no window and no administrator rights) and notes every 5 s which app is in the foreground. It stops counting after 5 min without input, except for a game or a full-screen video. It keeps only the app's name, never the window title, and everything stays on your PC. You can pause, stop recording an app, delete the history or turn it off. It doesn't open the process of any game.
- **Usage by app (Internet → Connection):** how much each app and game downloaded and uploaded today and over the last 7, 30 or 60 days. The **Map** shows each app sized by its traffic (games highlighted), the **Pie** shows the top 4 and the rest together, with each one's percentage, and **Per day** shows download and upload side by side with the average; clicking an app shows its usage day by day. The numbers are the ones Windows already keeps (the same as Settings → Network & internet → Data usage), so the screen opens with the history of the last 60 days. Nothing to turn on, nothing keeps running and no app is opened.
- **Errors (Windows → Maintenance):** blue screens (with the code explained), sudden shutdowns, the graphics driver that crashed and recovered and the apps that closed on their own or stopped responding, by month and by app. It shows the file where each app crashed; when it's the graphics driver, the screen says that updating the driver usually fixes it. Each event has **Details**: where it came from in the Windows log, the full time and the fields (app, version, file where it crashed and error code with what it means; for a blue screen, the code and the parameters), with **Copy details** to post on a forum (without your account name in the paths). It only reads the Windows logs.
- **Windows Update and drivers (Windows → Maintenance):** the last Windows update, what failed (with the code explained and whether it installed later), whether a restart is pending, the version and age of the graphics driver and the devices with problems (disabled ones show up separately). To install, the button opens Windows Update. In **Control**, you can **pause** updates for 7, 14 or 35 days, **not let Windows swap the drivers** you install from the manufacturer (NVIDIA, AMD, Intel) and **stay on the current version** of Windows (getting only fixes and security updates, with a warning when it's close to the end of support). Both options have Undo and show up in Windows → Changes; the pause has Resume. **Delivery Optimization** is there too: download updates only from Microsoft and from the PCs on your network, without sending pieces to other PCs over the internet. If one of them was already on before PCManager, you can turn it off too: it asks and goes back to the Windows default.
- **Organize (Storage):** tidies up the loose files in the Downloads folder (and others you add) with rules: by type or extension, name, age, size and even the **site the file was downloaded from** ("what came from Discord goes to folder X"). Each rule moves files to a folder (directly, by month or by type) or to the Recycle Bin. It comes with suggested rules, all unchecked. You check the ones you want, see the **preview** file by file and only then click **Organize**. It never runs on its own, never deletes for good, never overwrites a file (it becomes "name (2)") and doesn't touch downloads in progress. **Undo** moves back what the last run moved.
- **Boot (Windows → Performance):** how long the PC takes to start on each boot, separating the motherboard BIOS from Windows, with the average of the last 30 days, the unexpected shutdowns (power loss, crash) and which boots used Windows fast startup. The numbers come from the logs Windows already keeps, and time spent on the sign-in screen doesn't count. If the BIOS takes longer than Windows, the screen says so (that's adjusted in the BIOS; the app doesn't touch it). **Turn off fast startup** (with Undo) makes the PC really shut down, which fixes a driver stuck after "shutting down"; with hibernation off, fast startup no longer exists.
- **Last opened and "Unused for 90 days" (Apps → Installed):** each app shows when it was last opened, from what Windows itself records (and from Usage time, if it's on). The **Unused for 90 days** filter gathers the ones you haven't opened in months, with their size, so you can decide what to uninstall. "No usage record" means Windows didn't keep any launch.
- **Updates (Apps):** lists the apps with a new version through **winget**, Windows' own package manager, and updates them one by one or with **Update all**. Each one downloads from the official source and installs without prompts; close the app first. Nothing updates on its own or during a game.
- **Startup (Apps → Startup):** what opens with Windows (registry, Startup folder and scheduled tasks), the impact of each one on the last boot (the same calculation as Task Manager) and the items that point to apps that no longer exist. The switch turns them on and off like Task Manager, without deleting anything. **Add an app** sets an `.exe` to open when you sign in to Windows (only for your account). **Remove** takes it off the list and keeps a copy: in **Removed**, **Restore** puts it back as it was. Scheduled tasks and anti-cheats can only be turned off, and turning off an anti-cheat asks for confirmation.
- **Checkup and Optimization (Windows → Checkup and Games → Optimization, or the button on Home and in Disks):** a list of what's fine and what can be improved: free space, disk health, junk to clean, Recycle Bin, startup apps, startup time, app and Windows updates, crashes in the last month and apps slowing down the PC right now. It doesn't change anything: each item has a button that takes you to the right screen, where you decide. In **Games → Optimization**, it checks the Windows settings that matter most in a game: a monitor below its maximum refresh rate (a 144 Hz one running at 60 Hz, for example), a power saving plan on a PC without a battery, Windows Game Mode, Game Bar background recording, an old graphics driver, GPU scheduling and mouse acceleration. Each item opens the Windows screen where you change it.
- **Copy report (Windows → Checkup, or Ctrl K):** puts together in text the PC's parts, the graphics driver, the health and space of the disks, Windows Update and the Checkup result, to paste when someone is going to help you (Discord, forum). Before copying, it shows exactly what goes; it doesn't include file names, your account name in paths or IP addresses, and nothing is sent: it only goes to the clipboard.
- **Activity (Apps):** the apps open right now, with the memory and CPU each one uses (the ones with the same name, like Chrome tabs, are added up). **Close** asks the app to close, like clicking the X, and it may ask whether you want to save; if it doesn't close, **Force** ends it right away, with confirmation. Games, anti-cheats (Vanguard, EAC, BattlEye…) and Windows parts show a lock and are never closed from here. The list doesn't open any app, not even games. Nothing is closed on its own. At the top, memory and CPU with the line of the last 2 minutes. Pick **Memory** or **CPU** and the whole screen follows: the **Donut** shows the 4 apps that use the most, the others and what's free; the **Map** shows each app sized by what it uses (the shade shows CPU); and **Over time** shows the last 2 minutes.
- **Alert when an app hogs resources (Background):** starts off. When on, the background service checks the app list every 10 s and sends a Windows notification when an app spends 2 minutes in a row above 50% CPU or 40% memory, or when the PC's memory goes over 90%. Never during a game or full screen, never about Windows parts, anti-cheats or games, and at most one warning per app per hour. It only warns, it doesn't close anything; the latest ones stay in **Apps → Activity → Recent alerts**.
- **Repairs (Windows → Maintenance; the internet ones are in Internet → Fixes):** quick fixes with Windows' own tools, each with the ⓘ showing the command: **Renew the connection** (connected, but no internet), **Reset the network** (`netsh winsock reset` and `netsh int ip reset`; a static IP and custom DNS go back to automatic, Wi-Fi passwords stay, and it asks you to restart the PC), **Clear the DNS cache**, **Restart Explorer** (stuck taskbar), **Rebuild the icon cache**, **Check Windows files** (DISM and `sfc /scannow`, 10 to 30 min, can be canceled) and open **System Restore**. It runs only when you click, one at a time, and never during a game.
- **PC Run:** during long waits (first disk scan, Cleanup, Duplicates, Compress, Update all and Check Windows files), after 15 seconds **Play while you wait** shows up: a small game where the PCManager monitor jumps over `.tmp` files, folders and bugs. Space, ↑ or click to jump; Esc closes. It never opens on its own, the wait keeps going underneath and, when it ends, "Done" shows up without interrupting the round. The high score stays on your PC.
- **Old drivers (Storage → Cleanup):** Windows keeps every driver version ever installed, and graphics driver ones are often hundreds of MB each. The screen groups them by driver and marks what's **in use**, the **newest** and the **old** ones. **Remove** only accepts the old ones no device uses, uses Windows' own `pnputil` without forcing (it refuses to delete what's in use) and, before that, creates a restore point; if Windows doesn't create the restore point, nothing is removed, and you choose whether to continue without it.
- **What's new (Settings):** what changed in each version, split into New, Improved and Fixed, one change per line, with the installed version open. Also through **What changed** in About and through Ctrl K. After an update, Notifications tell you once, with the link to it. The texts come with the app; nothing goes to the internet.
- **Components (Settings):** you choose which PC parts PCManager can access: **HDD/SSD**, **CPU**, **Memory**, **Graphics card**, **Network** and **Windows**. A part that's off isn't read or changed, and whatever depends on it disappears from the app. Without HDD/SSD, for example, Disks, Files, Cleanup and Installed disappear. Everything starts on until you change it. Turning a part off doesn't delete what was already collected; for that, use **Data already collected**, right below. With **Windows** off, the app doesn't know whether a game is open, so it stops automatic scans and warns before heavy actions. The check for a new PCManager version has its own switch in **About**.
- **Changes (Windows):** everything PCManager changed that still has the previous value stored, in one place: Performance (tweaks, services, tasks and boot), Privacy, Personalization, Keyboard, OneDrive, Windows Update, DNS, Laptop, keyboard shortcuts, what left Startup and the removed Windows apps. Each line says **since when** and whether it's **in effect** or whether **Windows undid it** (usually in an update), and has **Undo** (or Restore / Reinstall from Microsoft Store). When Windows undoes something, a notice at the top offers **Apply again** (writes it again and keeps the value from before PCManager stored) or **Leave it as Windows set it** (forgets the stored value; refuses if Windows undid only part of the tweak). **Undo everything** shows how many items per screen and what's left out, asks for confirmation, goes back one by one and shows how many came back and why the rest didn't. Swapped drivers are in a separate block, with **Open in Drivers**. What was already set before PCManager doesn't show up. The tweak screens have, at the bottom, the link "See everything PCManager changed", and Ctrl K has **Undo everything** (opens the confirmation, without undoing anything on its own).
- **Tasks (Windows → Performance):** Windows scheduled tasks in three groups. **From apps:** the name, the app (the one from Installed, when possible), **when it runs** in words ("at sign-in and every day at 14:37") and the last run with its result; warnings for "app no longer exists" and, on updaters, "turned off, the app stops updating itself". The switch turns them on and off (the previous state is stored and shows up in Changes with **Undo**), and **Run now** asks for confirmation. If the app turns its own task back on, the line shows it and offers **Apply again**. **Protected** (anti-cheat, like Vanguard) and **From Windows** only show up. It reads Windows Task Scheduler, never deletes or creates a task and nothing changes with a game open.
- **Sensors (Hardware):** on the board, **Edit** opens it as a draft, which is only saved when you click **Save**: the top bar has icons only (hover to see the name) with **➕ Add**, **Undo** and **Redo** (**Ctrl+Z** and **Ctrl+Y**), the **Board size**, the style (Instruments or Cards), the six colors, **Reset to default**, **Cancel** (with changes, it asks "Discard the changes?") and **Save**. The board is made of rows: each piece takes 1/4, 1/3, half, 2/3 or the whole row, and the rows split the board's height (a chart needs more than a number), with nothing left over and no scrolling. While editing, each piece gets a **strip**: drag it by the handle to move it (a violet line shows where the piece lands, and Esc cancels), click the name to give it a nickname, pick the width in the small bars or drag the right edge (it snaps), switch between chart, bar and gauge and remove it with the **✕**. With the keyboard: arrows move, Shift+←/→ changes the width, Delete removes. The **Add** palette floats over the board: **Templates** (Default, the **Case panel**, made for wide small screens like 1920 × 480, and **Group by part**, which arranges your board in blocks keeping the order), **Build** (**blocks**, **value rows** and **titles**) and **Sensors**, with search; click to add or drag to the exact spot. The space left in a row shows **+ Add here**, and the value row has a **+** on each side to add a sensor at the start or at the end. **Board size** picks **This screen**, a small LCD screen (1920 × 480, 1280 × 480, 800 × 480), **16:9 TV**, **Portrait 9:16** or a size of your own: the board is drawn at that size and shrinks or grows as a whole to fit, aligned to the top (**Fill the screen** stretches it). In the screen bar, **⬇ Export** saves your board to a file to send to a friend, and **⬆ Import** opens a PCManager board or a Rainmeter skin (.ini): cards become blocks, numbers side by side become value rows and graphs become charts. For the skin's HWiNFO sensors, leave HWiNFO open when you import. A board from another PC opens with the sensors swapped for this PC's equivalents (the graphics card, the CPU, the memory, the disks and the network); the ones with no equivalent, like HWiNFO ones when it isn't here, are left out, and a notice shows which before opening (**Cancel** or **Open the board**). The default already comes in blocks by part (graphics card, CPU and system), and **Group by part** arranges your board the same way, keeping the order you left. With **HWiNFO** open and the Gadget export on, turn on **HWiNFO sensors** to also get the CPU temperature and power draw and the graphics card hotspot (the app only reads the registry; the driver belongs to HWiNFO). **⛶** puts the board in full screen on the monitor the app is on; **🖥️→** opens the sensors window, with a board of its own, straight in full screen on another monitor (with only one monitor, in a window that can stay **always on top**). In full screen on the other monitor, **Minimize** (arrows pointing down) minimizes the sensors window with one click; it comes back through the taskbar or through 🖥️→. In full screen the bar only shows up when you move the mouse and disappears after 3 s; Esc exits, and the screen doesn't turn off (it's a normal window: nothing shows up inside the game). Clicking the **title of a block** opens the **part details**: all its sensors, with minimum, maximum and the chart, and **+** to add to the board; the **💬** (when you hover over a piece or each sensor in the details) asks the assistant, if it's on, to explain that number in a balloon, with **Continue in chat**; the expand icon (or **Details**, in the bar) opens the page with all the parts side by side and search. It only reads, without installing a driver, so **it doesn't show the CPU temperature**. It only measures with the screen open. To see the graphics card's peak during a match: in **⚙️ Sensor options**, turn on **Keep measuring with the window hidden**, play and come back. Or turn on **Settings → System tray**: the X then hides the app near the clock, the sensors measure all the time, and in the details and in **Details** the chart also shows the **last 10 minutes** or the **last hour** (the peak of every 10 s). The icon shows the temperature and usage when you hover over it and, if you want, a number drawn on it (amber at 80 °C and red at 90 °C). To close it completely, use **Quit** in the icon's menu. **Export CSV** (in ⚙️ Sensor options) saves the numbers.
- **Drivers (Hardware):** **Search** asks Windows Update which drivers exist for the PC's devices and shows, for each one, the current one (version, date and manufacturer) next to the offered one. **Newer driver on Windows Update** lists what's worth updating; **Check first** warns when the offered one is **older than yours**, **changes manufacturer** (the ASUS driver for a generic one, for example) or can't be compared; the graphics card reminds you that the manufacturer's app usually has a newer one. Check them and click **Install**: PCManager first creates a **restore point** (if System Protection is off, it offers to turn it on), downloads and installs them one by one and, if any asks for it, offers **Restart now**. Each swap stays in **Swapped by PCManager** for 30 days, with **Roll back**. It doesn't install with a game open or with "Don't swap drivers" on. In Settings → Background you can turn on the search **once a week** (it tells you in Notifications; it never installs on its own).
- **Game sessions (Games → Sessions):** starts off. When on (here or in Settings → Background), the service notes each time you play (a known game opened, or any app that stays 10 minutes in full screen: those show up with the **full screen** badge, because it may be a video) and, every 5 s during the session, the usage and temperature of the graphics card, the CPU and the memory. Each session shows how long it lasted, averages and peaks, the ping (if Stability is on), charts and the comparison with your other sessions of the same game; you can filter by game and see 7, 30 or 90 days. The game isn't opened or touched (only the Windows and graphics card counters, as in Sensors), nothing is read without a game open, and everything stays on this PC for 90 days. Optional: **show a summary when the game closes**. It can't measure FPS or the CPU temperature. After two matches of at least 10 min, each game gets **Suggestions**: whether the graphics card is the limit (lower the quality or turn on DLSS/FSR), whether the CPU holds it back (close apps, lower the settings that load it), whether the graphics card and CPU have room to spare (it's an FPS limit or V-Sync), whether VRAM or memory filled up, whether the graphics card got hot, whether the internet stuttered, and whether the game is on a hard drive or on an almost full disk. Each one shows the numbers behind it. At the top of Sessions you see the **Games with suggestions**, and Games → Optimization has a line with them. The game isn't read: these are the PC's numbers during the match.
- **Services (Windows → Performance):** shows the optional Windows services (a closed list, each with what stops working) and those of other apps (updaters, RGB, launchers), with how much CPU, memory and disk each one uses right now. The **heavy** badge marks the ones above 1% CPU, 1 MB/s of disk or 200 MB of memory. **Start only when needed** lets Windows start it when some app asks; **Disable** doesn't. **Undo** (there or in Windows → Changes) puts it back as it was. Anti-cheats and antivirus get a lock, and the rest of the Windows services are left out. Turn on **Service load** in Background to also see the last 7 days, and find the ones that are heavy only now and then.
- **Laptop (Hardware; only shows up on a laptop):** PCManager recognizes a laptop by its battery and its chassis type (a UPS on a desktop PC doesn't count).
  - **Laptop → Graphics card** (with two cards): the games on the disk and the card each one uses. **Use the dedicated card** writes the same preference as Settings → Display → Graphics and takes effect the next time the game opens. The card for the manufacturer's app (Armoury Crate, Legion, OMEN, MSI Center…) says what to look for there: Turbo profile, MUX and charge limit.
  - **Laptop → Power:** separate power mode plugged in and on battery, CPU turbo when plugged in (or **Run cooler**, which turns turbo off to run cooler), Wi-Fi without power saving when plugged in (fewer ping spikes) and at which percentage Energy saver turns on.
  - **Battery:** the **Battery** part in Components starts **off**; the app only reads the battery after you turn it on (the screen and Notifications remind you). When on: the charge right now (in W), the warning when the charger can't keep up and when you play on battery, and the health: how much of its factory capacity the battery still holds and how many cycles it has.
  - **Laptop → Heat:** the CPU speed compared to its base and how far Windows lets it go. When the laptop holds the CPU back, the screen says the likely reason (heat, power or laptop limit) and gives tips to run cooler.
  - **Game sessions, Notifications and Checkup:** each match notes how long it was on battery and with the CPU held back; Notifications tell you ("40 min on battery", "CPU held back for 10 min"); and Checkup gets the laptop items (games on the integrated card, power saving mode when plugged in, turbo, Wi-Fi, battery health and charger). In Performance, the tweaks made for desktop PCs get the **Laptop: best avoided** badge.
  - In the battery **Health**, how long it lasts on a full charge at the current rate of use (from the Windows estimate).
  - Every tweak has an ⓘ, shows the previous value, has Undo and shows up in Windows → Changes. No game is opened or touched.
- **PCManager right now (Windows → Performance → Tweaks, at the top):** how much CPU and memory PCManager itself is using, adding up the window, the screen (WebView2), the background monitor, the assistant while it answers and the tools it opened. **Show the parts** shows each one. It reads the same list as Task Manager, every 3 s and only with the screen open; no process is opened to measure.
- **Performance tweaks (Windows → Performance):** global Windows tweaks, one by one, each with an honest badge (**Real gain**, **Small gain**, **Almost nothing** or **Preference**) and the warning of when not to use it. Turning one on stores the previous value and turning it off goes back to it. If it was already set before PCManager, **already set** shows up and there's nothing to undo. The **Recommended** profile turns on only what has a real gain. Also here: **turn off hibernation** (frees the space of `hiberfil.sys`, which Cleanup points to with a button that comes here; it asks for confirmation on a laptop) and **don't let Windows turn off USB ports** to save power (the mouse and headset don't disappear), both with Undo. In the **Memory** tab, you can empty the standby cache (it rarely helps, and the screen explains why). In the **DNS** tab, the app measures the servers from your PC, shows how many ms faster each one is than your current DNS and says whether it's worth switching (under 10 ms, it isn't), and switches with one click: sites open faster, but **it doesn't change your in-game ping**, and the chosen server can see which sites you visit. **Restore** goes back to the previous DNS. With a game open, nothing changes.
- **Privacy (Windows → Preferences):** turns on and off, one by one, Windows ads, suggestions and extra data: advertising ID, suggestions in Start, Windows tips, suggestions in Settings, fun facts on the lock screen, "suggested" apps that install themselves, Bing in Start search, search history on the PC, cloud search (OneDrive, Outlook), online speech recognition, collection of what you type and handwrite, feedback requests, notifications on the lock screen, optional diagnostic data, the Copilot button, Recall snapshots, Widgets, Microsoft account notices in Start and activity history (and, depending on the Windows version, Click to Do or News and interests). Nothing is on by default; with a game open, nothing changes. Each item shows, under **Technical**, the key and the value it writes; turning one on stores what was there before and turning it off goes back to it; what was already set before PCManager shows up as **already set** and can't be turned off from here. **Reset everything to the Windows default** deletes all the values, including those. What changes the taskbar and Start shows up after restarting Explorer (the button is in Repairs).
- **Personalization (Windows → Preferences):** Windows preferences with on/off switches, in four sections. **File Explorer:** show **file extensions**, show **hidden files**, open to **This PC**, **right-click with the full menu** (without Windows 11's "Show more options") and checkboxes. **Taskbar and Start:** **icons on the left**, **"End task"** on right-click, **seconds on the clock**, never combine windows, no recent items and, on a laptop, the battery percentage. **Windows and appearance:** **dark mode** (switches right away), **menus without delay** and **Alt+Tab without Edge tabs**. **Desktop and clipboard:** **This PC on the desktop** and **clipboard history** (Win+V). Turning one on stores what was there and turning it off goes back to it; what was already set shows up as **already set**.
- **OneDrive (Windows → Preferences):** shows whether OneDrive is running, which of your folders are inside it and how many files are only in the cloud. Three tweaks, with Undo: **don't start with Windows**, **remove from the File Explorer sidebar** and **block it completely** (the Windows policy that stops OneDrive from running and syncing). Before blocking, the app tells you how many of your folders are inside OneDrive and how many files become unreachable (the cloud-only ones), and says where, in OneDrive itself, to bring the folders back. Nothing is deleted, uninstalled or moved.
- **Keyboard (Windows → Preferences):** Windows shortcuts that get in the way mid-game, each with an on/off switch: the **Sticky Keys** prompt (Shift 5 times), the **Filter Keys** prompt (right Shift 8 s) and the **Toggle Keys** prompt (Num Lock 5 s); the feature stays in Settings, only the prompt goes away. Also **Alt+Shift and Ctrl+Shift switching the keyboard language** (takes effect when you sign in to Windows again; Win+Space keeps working) and what **Print Screen** does. Under **At startup**, **Num Lock on** at the sign-in screen and in your account. Turning one on stores what was there and turning it off goes back to it; nothing keeps listening to the keyboard or presses keys for you. Optimization (Games) warns if the Sticky Keys shortcut is on. In **Swap keys** (takes effect after restarting the PC): turn off the **Windows key**, **Caps Lock as Ctrl**, turn off **Caps Lock** or **Insert**, through Windows' own feature (Scancode Map); swaps made by other apps stay as they are. In **Shortcuts to open apps**: pick an app and a combination (Ctrl+Alt, Ctrl+Shift or Alt+Shift with a letter, number or F1–F12), and PCManager creates a shortcut with a key in the Start menu (the `PCManager - Atalhos` folder); Windows itself opens the app, with nothing in the background.
- **Windows apps (Apps → Installed):** the Microsoft Store apps of your account, to remove the ones that come with Windows and you don't use (News, Weather, Tips, Solitaire, Clipchamp, Maps…). Nothing comes checked: **Select the suggestions** checks only the closed list of Microsoft apps; what you installed (Spotify, WhatsApp…) stays under **Other**. The Store, winget, Xbox and Game Pass, Edge, codecs and Windows parts get a lock. It removes them only from your account, and each removed app gets **Reinstall from Microsoft Store**.
- **Disk health (Storage → Disks):** click **Check now** and the app reads what each disk records about its own health (temperature, SSD lifespan, a hard drive's bad sectors, power-on hours) and Windows' own assessment. Each disk gets **Good**, **Warning** ("back up and keep an eye on it") or **Bad** ("back up now"), and the screen shows what got worse since the previous check. It only reads when you click, never during a game, and doesn't write anything to the disk. External USB drives often don't report their health: "No data" shows up.
- **History:** what grew in the last 24 h, 7 days, 30 days or since the beginning, by category and by folder, with the chart of used space. PCManager takes a snapshot of the disk every 6 hours while it's open, so the history starts on the day you install it. The overview also shows "+X GB in the last 7 days".
- **Low space alert:** when a disk has less than 10% free (adjust it in Settings; 0 turns it off), Windows shows a notification, at most once a day, and never during a game.
- **Export CSV:** the lists (search, filters, Apps and Suggestions) have **Export CSV**, which opens in Excel.
- **Back and forward:** the mouse side buttons (or **Alt + ←** and **Alt + →**) go back and forward like in a browser, between screens and between folders in Files.
- **● live:** the index updates itself while you use the PC. **● static** means the disk doesn't allow it (flash drive, network); use **Rescan** whenever you want to refresh.

## Games and anti-cheat

- Day to day, PCManager only reads file names, sizes and dates. File contents are only read when you ask: in the Duplicates search (only in your folders, never in games) and to Compress a game, always with the game closed. It never opens a game's process, doesn't install any driver of its own (Windows Update drivers only when you ask, in Hardware → Drivers) and doesn't read the memory of any app.
- While a game is open (or an app in full screen), it goes into **game mode**: it doesn't start full scans and almost stops live mode. A requested scan stays "waiting for the game to close". Cleanup, Duplicates and Compress don't start, and if they're already running, they stop or pause until the game closes.
- Before each version is published, it's tested open and scanning alongside Riot Vanguard and Easy Anti-Cheat.

## Assistant (optional)

If you have **Claude Code** or **Codex** (from OpenAI) installed and signed in on this PC, you can ask questions in plain language: "how do I free up 50 GB?", "why did the PC get hot in the last match?", "what makes the PC slow to start?". Turn it on in **Settings → Assistant**; a chat button shows up in the bottom right corner. With a folder or file selected in Files, the question takes its data along.

- Right below "Turn on the assistant", in **What the assistant can read**, you choose the blocks: Storage (starts on), Apps, Internet, Hardware and games, and Windows. It only looks at what's on, and each answer shows **Checked: …** with what was read.
- The questions and the data of the blocks that are on (names, paths, sizes, apps, internet usage, sensors and sessions) go to Anthropic through your Claude Code (or to OpenAI through your Codex), using your account. File contents are never sent, except for the Game files block (below).
- The assistant only reads and chats: it doesn't delete, doesn't change settings and doesn't open anything. Claude Code runs without tools and without plugins; PCManager is the one that reads the data.
- **Codex:** in **Who answers**, pick Codex; the first time, a notice says the data goes to OpenAI. Codex has no tool-free mode like Claude Code. PCManager turns off the features it knows can take action (commands, images, browser, computer control, plugins, apps) and, before each question, checks with Codex itself: a feature PCManager doesn't recognize that shows up turned on is turned off too, and if any stays on, Codex doesn't run; editing files is blocked by read-only mode; and Codex is stopped when it says it's going to use a tool. MCP servers a company sets up on the PC aren't turned off. It doesn't give the same guarantee as Claude Code. If a feature doesn't turn off, it doesn't run and says which one. If there's an error, send the error text. To install: the official Codex CLI installer or `npm install -g @openai/codex`, then `codex` and sign in (the Codex app and the VS Code extension on their own aren't enough).
- **Game files** (starts off, with its own notice): the assistant can ask for the video and game config files of VALORANT, Fortnite, Counter-Strike 2, Apex Legends, Overwatch 2, Rocket League, Rainbow Six Siege, GTA V and Minecraft, only from your user folders (never from the folder where the game is installed). PCManager only reads, with the game closed, and never writes, renames or deletes anything; names, accounts and IDs go as (hidden). Since many games ban players who touch these files by hand, the assistant suggests changes through the game's menu.
- On a laptop, the **Laptop** block shows up in **What the assistant can read**: it looks at the battery (with the Battery part on), the power modes and each game's graphics card.
- With the assistant on, the Maintenance events, the updates that failed and the devices with problems have a balloon: it opens the chat and already asks why that happened, sending that item's data.
- Without Claude Code or Codex, the assistant simply doesn't show up.

## Privacy

- Network only reads the totals Windows already keeps per app. It doesn't see sites, content or addresses.
- The app only connects to the internet to check for updates (on GitHub), to download a new version of an app when you click Update (through winget), to measure DNS servers when you click Test servers, through your Claude Code if you turn on the assistant, with small pings to your router and to 1.1.1.1 (Cloudflare) if you turn on **Internet stability**, with `speed.cloudflare.com` when you click **Test now** in the Speed test, and through Windows Update when you run **Check Windows files** in Repairs (DISM may download good copies of Windows files).
- The app runs as administrator, so what it uses to act (disk index, settings, what can be undone, logs) is in `C:\ProgramData\PCManager\<your account>`, a folder only an administrator can change (you can only read it). What the background counter and the assistant write (Usage time, Game sessions, Stability, the assistant's folder) is in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\HDManager`. The assistant, the counter and the uninstaller of an app from your account (or in a folder any app can change) run as you, without administrator rights.

## Updates

When a new version comes out, a small notice shows up in the corner of the window. It's only installed if you click **Install and restart**.

## Uninstall

Windows Settings → **Apps** → **PCManager** → Uninstall. To also delete the index, the settings and the logs, delete the folders `C:\ProgramData\PCManager` (Windows asks for administrator rights) and `%LOCALAPPDATA%\HDManager`.

Send me the most recent log file from the `C:\ProgramData\PCManager` folder, in the folder that starts with `S-1-5-21`, inside `logs` (the exact path shows up in Settings → About; paste it into the File Explorer address bar).
